# Problem Hook & Value Proposition (Module 1)

## Responses
- **Chosen path, which Riverty business-unit case are you using?:** BU Collection: A Fair Way Forward. Product concept: Back in Flow, a digital instalment journey that helps consumers agree to a plan they can keep.
- **Strategic crisis, based on the brief, if the company does nothing for 12 months, what happens to its market position?:** The company will keep reporting a rising Self-Service Rate (looks great at a first glance) while about one in five new instalment plans breaks within 60 days. Clients will see the broken plans as complaints in their reporting and question Riverty as a collection partner, purchased portfolios will lose value, and agents will absorb the failures as costlier work. The north star will tell a success story the outcomes don’t support.
- **Moment of misery, what is the user likely doing outside the product (Google, spreadsheets, a competitor) because your tool isn’t serving them?:** The user is currently forced to leave the portal and call an agent, because she doesn’t understand why income is asked, can only see one claim at a time, and fears a wrong answer will be used against her. On the phone she repeats everything she already entered online. If she pushes through alone instead, she picks the shortest plan because it looks right, and misses the second payment.
- **Problem hook, summarize the business risk and user pain into one urgent sentence.:** We must solve a north star that rewards broken plans, with SSR rising while one in five new plans misses a payment within 60 days, by addressing consumers who are pushed to agree quickly instead of agreeing to a plan they understand and can actually keep.
- **Value proposition, based on the initiative in your assigned Riverty case, what is the new value and why is it urgent to launch now?:** For consumers with one or more open claims and irregular income, we will show exactly what they owe across all claims and what happens if something goes wrong, before they commit, so they choose a plan they can keep. It’s urgent because every month at today’s baseline adds about 730 broken plans that the current north star doesn’t show.

## Cold-read your own hook
- **Is the business risk high enough to justify a new initiative, a high-stakes threat, or a minor inconvenience?:** It’s a high-stakes threat, not an inconvenience. Broken plans affect recovery, client trust and portfolio returns at the same time, and the leading metric hides the problem, so it won’t fix itself.
- **Is the moment of misery a systemic problem or just an edge case?:** Systemic. The top three agent contact reasons (missed payment consequences, combining claims, why income is asked) make up 52% of contacts. Missed payments rose from 15.2% to 19.0% over six months, and multi-claim consumers (about a third of starters) agree far less often.
- **Does the value proposition actually remove the obstacle you identified, or is it a generic feature?:** Yes, because it targets the two failure points directly: uncertainty before commitment and plans that don’t fit the consumer’s real income. A generic feature would be “a better chat” or “a faster flow,” which would make the speed trap worse.
