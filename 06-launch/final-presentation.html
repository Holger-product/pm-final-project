Strategy

Problem hook

We must solve a north star that rewards broken plans, with SSR rising while one in five new plans misses a payment within 60 days, by addressing consumers who are pushed to agree quickly instead of agreeing to a plan they understand and can actually keep.

Value proposition

For consumers with one or more open claims and irregular income, we will show exactly what they owe and what happens if something goes wrong, before they commit, so they choose a plan they can keep. It’s urgent because every month at today’s baseline adds about 730 broken plans that the current north star doesn’t show.

Data-backed hypothesis · Module 3

Based on consumers like N-02 who pick the plan that "looked like the right answer" and miss the second payment, and missed payments rising from 15.6% to 18.4% after the plan screen put the 6-month plan first (19.0% by Month 6), I believe that showing affordability fit and missed-payment consequences before confirmation for quick closers with variable income will result in more plans being kept, as measured by a drop in missed payments within 60 days from 19.0% to 15.5% or lower. I will protect the agreement rate at 44% or higher and will make a go/no-go decision after 6 weeks of enrollment and 60 days of observation.

MDE3 pp

Ship

If missed payments within 60 days drop by at least 3 percentage points at p < 0.05, the agreement rate stays at 45% or higher, kept plans per starter are not lower than control, and agent contact within 7 days stays at 33% or lower.

Iterate

If missed payments drop significantly but by less than 3 points, or drop by 3 points or more without reaching significance; we do not extend the test, we redesign and retest.

Kill

If missed payments show no improvement or rise, or if the agreement rate falls below 45% or kept plans per starter fall below control, because then the result comes from filtering people out, not from better plans.

Slide 10 · Module 6

Riverty BU Collection

Story

Aha moment

The same trap kept reappearing in different forms. SSR counts a plan as resolved when it’s created, the chat lifts agreements while plans break more often, and even my own experiment metric could have been gamed by filtering people out.

Every good metric needed a second number to keep it honest.

Friction

The course pace left little room to discuss and challenge conclusions, so the real work was making sure AI output stayed honest to the data.

Key takeaways

Speed and motivation were the trap in this case, and clarity was the value. AI was strongest at synthesis and execution, and weakest at noticing when a metric could be gamed.

What I would do next

  • Run the A/B test
  • Add a third arm to separate the plan ordering effect from the fit check
  • Test the pay day date (B6)
  • Shadow agents to find the workarounds no dashboard shows

PRD highlights

Plan Fit Check, Simplified PRD (Riverty BU Collection, A Fair Way Forward)

Scenario: Riverty BU Collection, A Fair Way Forward

Author: Me · Status: Draft · Target: High-Fidelity Prototype · Persona: The quick closer: a consumer with variable shift income and one or more claims who wants it done fast and commits without checking fit or consequences.

1. The Big Picture

  • Vision: Every consumer leaves the portal with a plan that fits their real income and knows what happens if something goes wrong.
  • Press release: Starting today, consumers who set up an instalment plan with Riverty no longer have to guess which plan is the “right answer.” Plan Fit Check asks three short questions about a normal month, a low month and fixed costs, explains why, and then shows which plans fit, which are tight, and which are risky, with a clear reason for each.

    Before anyone confirms, they see exactly what happens if a payment is missed. For consumers like Mara, who works shifts and used to pick the shortest plan only to miss the second payment, that means agreeing to something she can keep and then getting on with her life.

  • Success metric: Missed payments within 60 days as a share of agreements, from 19.0% to 15.5% or lower.
  • Guardrail: Agreement rate at 44% or higher.
User stories
  • As a consumer with variable income, I want to enter both a normal and a low month, so that the plans reflect what I can pay when money is tight. Acceptance: the lower value drives the labels, and people with a steady income can mark it as the same every month.
  • As a consumer in a hurry, I want to see at a glance which plans fit my budget, so that I don’t pick a plan just because it looks like the right answer. Acceptance: every plan shows a label and a one-line reason, best fit first.
  • As a consumer about to commit, I want to know what happens if I miss a payment, so that I’m not surprised later. Acceptance: the confirm button stays disabled until the consequences for the selected plan have been opened.
Screens to build
  • 1. Entry point, “Your Monthly Budget”: three number fields (normal month, low month, fixed costs), a “why we ask” note under each, a “same every month” option, the open amount, and a Show my plans button.
  • 2. Feature core, “Plans That Fit You”: plan options (3, 6, 12 and 18 months) with monthly amount, Fits / Tight / Risky label, one-line reason, budget bar, and an expandable “What if I miss a payment?” panel for the selected plan.
  • 3. Success, “Your Plan Is Set Up”: summary of monthly amount, number of payments, first due date and fit, missed-payment rules, and a “Talk to an agent” link.
Functional requirements
  • The budget form has no more than three fields and can be completed in under 60 seconds.
  • Remaining budget is calculated as the low-month income minus fixed costs.
  • Each plan is labeled Fits if the instalment is up to 40% of the remaining budget, Tight from 40% to 70%, and Risky above 70% (prototype thresholds, not policy).
  • Plans are sorted best fit first, shortest first within the same label, and no plan is preselected.
  • Every label shows a reason, for example “€100 is 33% of what’s left after fixed costs.”
  • The confirm button stays disabled until the missed-payment panel has been opened for the currently selected plan. Switching plans resets this.
  • Choosing a Risky plan triggers a second confirmation that repeats the reason.
  • The confirmation screen shows monthly amount, number of payments, first due date and missed-payment rules.
Smart behaviors (Situation → Outcome)
  • If remaining budget is zero or negative → no plan is labeled Fits, and a “talk to an agent” path is shown.
  • If all plans are Risky → the longest plan is listed first, with the agent option highlighted.
  • If low-month income is higher than normal-month income → show a gentle prompt to check the numbers, and allow continuing.
  • If income is marked as the same every month → the low-month field is filled from the normal month.
  • If the consumer switches plans after opening the panel → the confirm button locks again until the new plan’s panel is opened.
  • If the consumer selects a Risky plan → show a second confirmation with the reason.
  • If the consumer goes back to the budget screen → their answers are kept.
Technical constraints
  • One self-contained HTML file with inline CSS and plain JavaScript. No external APIs, no login, no database, no network calls, no data stored or sent anywhere. The open amount (€1,200) is sample data, plans have no fees or interest, and the first payment is due 14 days after confirmation. The missed-payment text is placeholder copy marked “sample text, to be confirmed by Legal.” Visual style follows the Riverty Classic Design Guidelines (Guidelines V2).
Features out
  • One plan for several claims (B1), pay day date picker (B6), document upload or skip (B5), chat assistant (B8), agent handoff summary (B4), real payment processing, real credit or affordability checks.
Edge cases & safety guard
  • Empty fields, non-numeric input, negative numbers, values above €20,000 a month, and fixed costs higher than income all show a clear inline message or the agent path instead of a misleading result. The prototype must never preselect the shortest or highest instalment, never present placeholder legal text as real policy, never store or send entered data, and never use shaming or threatening language.
Decision log
  • 1 Risky plans are warned about, not blocked: consumers keep the choice, which respects their autonomy and the fairness guardrail.
  • 2 The pay day picker is excluded from this version, so the A/B test isolates the effect of the fit check and the consequence panel.
Evals
  • Accuracy: 100% of plans in ten prepared test cases receive the correct label according to the 40% and 70% thresholds.
  • Time on task: median under 2 minutes from first input to confirmation in a hallway test.
  • Safety: in 100% of confirmations, the missed-payment panel for the confirmed plan was opened first, and no plan was ever preselected.

Control vs. Variant

  • Control (A): Today’s journey: no explanation of how income answers are used, the 6-month plan shown first, no fit labels or reasons, and confirmation possible without seeing what happens after a missed payment. This is where the quick closer picks the plan that “looked like the right answer” (N-02) and where missed payments rose from 15.6% to 18.4% after the plan screen change, reaching 19.0% by Month 6.
  • Variant (B): Plan Fit Check, tested as one bundled feature, with the result attributed to the feature as a whole. Screen 1, “Your Monthly Budget”: normal month, low month and fixed costs, a “why we ask” note under each (this includes the income explainer B2), and an “about the same every month” option. Screen 2, “Plans That Fit You”: the same plan lengths as control, labeled Fits (up to 40% of the remaining low-month budget), Tight (40% to 70%) or Risky (above 70%), each with a one-line reason, best fit first, nothing preselected. Confirmation stays locked until “What if I miss a payment?” has been opened for the selected plan, and resets when the plan changes. Risky plans trigger a second confirmation. Screen 3, “Your Plan Is Set Up”: amount, number of payments, first due date and missed-payment rules. Known limit: the variant also removes the 6-month-first ordering, which alone moved missed payments by about 3 points in Month 4, so the test can’t separate ordering from the fit check.
  • Held constant (isolation check): Outreach letters, emails and SMS. Eligibility rules and qualifying claims. Claim amounts, plan lengths, fees and interest. The chat assistant, available the same way in both arms. Reminder timing, including any fix for T-207, shipped to both arms or neither. The mobile upload fix (B5) ships to both arms before the test. The income explainer (B2) is part of the variant only and doesn’t ship separately until after the test. Agent scripts and the agent screen. Portal version apart from the variant screens. Pre-registered diagnostics, not decision metrics: share of confirmations on Risky plans, agent contacts about missed payments, and a directional read for shift workers only.

Hypothesis

I believe that Plan Fit Check for quick closers with variable shift income will result in more plans that fit a low month and are kept, as measured by a 3 percentage points change in missed payments within 60 days (share of new agreements) within 6 weeks of enrollment plus 60 days of observation. We will protect the agreement rate on started journeys throughout the test.

Shipping criteria

We will SHIP if missed payments within 60 days drop by at least 3 percentage points at p < 0.05, the agreement rate stays at 45% or higher, kept plans per starter are not lower than control, and agent contact within 7 days stays at 33% or lower. We will ITERATE if missed payments drop significantly but by less than 3 points, or drop by 3 points or more without reaching significance; we do not extend the test, we redesign and retest. We will KILL if missed payments show no improvement or rise, or if the agreement rate falls below 45% or kept plans per starter fall below control, because then the result comes from filtering people out, not from better plans. The read date is fixed at the end of the 60-day observation window, about 15 weeks after launch. Guardrails are checked weekly for harm only, and the test stops early if the variant’s agreement rate falls below 42%.

Goal & Audience

  • Goal: Conversion, The feature lives inside the existing journey, and consumers reach it through outreach that already exists, so awareness isn’t the problem. The goal is converting journey starters into plans that are actually kept, which is also what the M5 test measures.
  • Target audience: Primary: consumers in the German pilot who start an instalment journey, 8,360 per month (gtm_audience.csv), with quick closers on variable or shift income as the focus. Assumption: the data pack’s monthly volume represents the German pilot segment, since the brief leaves it open. Secondary: about 10 collection agents in the pilot team (assumption, based on roughly 500 agent hours a month of journey-related contacts), client account managers, and legal and compliance, because they have to explain, report and approve what consumers see.

Launch Tier

  • M, Targeted, One journey in one market, about 8,360 starters a month. No new revenue line, but recovery over the plan’s lifetime and client reporting are directly affected, with about 115 fewer broken plans a month at the MDE. Silence is risky on the internal and client side: agents who can’t explain a Risky label create more calls, and clients who see agreement numbers shift without context complain about their reporting (N-10). For consumers this is a release they meet inside the journey, with no marketing, since marketing to people in debt would be inappropriate. For agents, account managers and compliance it’s a launch.

Channels

  1. Owned: The portal journey itself, where the quick closer already is after the reminder, so the feature reaches her at the moment of misery without any extra message.
  2. Owned: The existing outreach letters, emails and SMS, with one added line after the test: “See which plan fits your budget before you agree.” Unchanged during the A/B test to keep the arms isolated.
  3. Owned: Agent team briefings and the agent workspace, plus client account manager reviews for clients, reaching each group through routines that already exist. No paid channels, because paid media aimed at consumers in debt would conflict with the fairness guardrail.

Enablement & Assets

Agents: a one-page explainer of how Fits, Tight and Risky are calculated, an FAQ for “why was my plan labeled Risky?” and “what happens if I miss a payment?”, and a 30-minute briefing. Client account managers: a one-pager on test design, metrics and guardrails, so clients can read changes in their reporting. Legal and compliance: the final missed-payment text replacing the prototype placeholder, and documentation of how labels are calculated, so recommendations are explainable (N-12). AI drafts the FAQs, one-pagers and result summaries, always reviewed by a person. AI is not used for the final legal text, for any decision about an individual consumer without human review, or for client conversations.
