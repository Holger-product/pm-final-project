# GTM Strategy & Success Dashboard (Module 6)

## Get your prior work ready
- **The feature you're launching from your M4 PRD, one feature, not a list:** Plan Fit Check (B3): affordability input, plans labeled Fits, Tight or Risky with a reason, best fit first, and confirmation locked until “What if I miss a payment?” has been opened for the selected plan.
- **What your M5 experiment will test your hypothesis, primary metric and ship / iterate rule, the evidence the launch rests on:** Whether Plan Fit Check lowers missed payments within 60 days by at least 3 points (19.0% to 16.0%) at p < 0.05, while the agreement rate stays at 45% or higher and kept plans per starter are not lower than control. SHIP above that bar, ITERATE if positive but smaller or not significant, KILL if no improvement or if the result comes from filtering people out.
- **Your persona pull your M2 persona, this anchors your audience:** The quick closer: a consumer with variable shift income and one or more claims who wants it done fast and commits without checking fit or consequences.

## Set your goal, then your audience
- **Primary GTM goal awareness · engagement · conversion, pick one:** Conversion
- **Why this goal? what makes this the right goal for this feature right now:** The feature lives inside the existing journey, and consumers reach it through outreach that already exists, so awareness isn’t the problem. The goal is converting journey starters into plans that are actually kept. That’s also what the M5 test measures, so the launch goal and the evidence line up.
- **Target audience the specific segment your goal implies, be precise:** Primary: consumers in the German pilot who start an instalment journey, 8,360 per month (gtm_audience.csv), with quick closers on variable or shift income as the focus within it. Assumption: the data pack’s monthly volume represents the German pilot segment, since the brief leaves the segment size open. Secondary, and the real audience of this launch: the collection agents in the pilot team, client account managers, and legal and compliance, because they have to explain, report and approve what consumers see. Assumption: about 10 agents in the pilot team, since journey-related contacts add up to roughly 500 agent hours a month (2,592 contacts at about 12 minutes each), the equivalent of about 4 full-time agents, plus their other cases.

## Size your launch
- **Launch tier S (minimal) · M (targeted) · L (multi-channel) · XL (full GTM):** M (targeted)
- **Justification reach + revenue impact + what silence would risk:** Reach: one journey in one market, about 8,360 starters a month. Revenue impact: no new revenue line, but recovery over the plan’s lifetime and client reporting are directly affected, with about 115 fewer broken plans a month at the MDE. Risk of silence: high on the internal and client side. If agents can’t explain why a plan is labeled Risky, consumers call more, and if clients see agreement numbers shift without context, it shows up as a complaint about their reporting (N-10). That justifies more than an S, but nothing here needs multi-channel marketing, so not an L.
- **Is this a launch or a release? does it need go-to-market, or can it just ship?:** Both, depending on the audience. For consumers it’s a release: they meet it inside the journey, and marketing to people in debt would be inappropriate. For agents, client account managers and compliance it’s a launch, because they need to understand, explain and sign off on it before day one.

## Choose your top three channels and plan assets
- **Channel 1 owned / earned / paid, and why it reaches your audience:** Owned: the portal journey itself. This is where the quick closer already is after the reminder, so the feature reaches her at exactly the moment of misery, without any extra message.
- **Channel 2 owned / earned / paid, and why:** Owned: the existing outreach letters, emails and SMS, with one added line after the test (“See which plan fits your budget before you agree”). Not changed during the A/B test, to keep the arms isolated.
- **Channel 3 owned / earned / paid, and why:** Owned: agent team briefings and the agent workspace, plus client account manager reviews for clients. Agents and account managers are reached through their existing routines, not new channels. No paid channels at all, because paid media aimed at consumers in debt would conflict with the fairness guardrail.
- **Enablement & assets what Sales / CS / Support need, plus the assets to build (one-pager, demo, etc.):** For agents: a one-page explainer of how Fits, Tight and Risky are calculated, an FAQ for “why was my plan labeled Risky?” and “what happens if I miss a payment?”, and a 30-minute briefing. For client account managers: a one-pager on the test design, metrics and guardrails, so clients can read changes in their reporting. For legal and compliance: the final missed-payment text, replacing the prototype’s placeholder, and documentation of how labels are calculated, so the recommendation is explainable to consumer and agent (N-12). Where AI helps: drafting the FAQs and one-pagers, and summarizing results for stakeholders, always reviewed by a person. Where AI is not used: the final legal text on missed-payment consequences, any decision about an individual consumer without human review (the responsible automation guardrail), and conversations with clients, where trust depends on a person.

## Define how you'll know it worked
- **Success metrics 2 to 3 metrics that match your GTM goal:** 1. Missed payments within 60 days: from 19.0% to 16.0% or lower.
2. Kept plans per journey starter: at least the baseline of 37.3%, aiming higher.
3. Agreement rate on started journeys: 45% or higher.
- **Bad signal to watch for e.g. high reach, zero signups = message-market mismatch:** Missed payments fall while the agreement rate falls too: plans look better because people are filtered out, not helped. A high share of Risky plans confirmed anyway: consumers don’t trust or understand the labels. More agent calls asking “why is my plan Risky?”: the message isn’t landing, which points to the explainer copy rather than the feature.
- **Most likely post-launch decision double-down · iterate · pivot · deprioritize, and what would trigger it:** Iterate. The bundle tackles a known effect, so a positive direction is likely, but whether it clears the 3-point bar while holding agreements is uncertain. The trigger for iterate is a positive result below 3 points, or a high rate of Risky confirmations. The trigger for double-down is a SHIP result: roll out to the full pilot, add the pay day date test, and extend to purchased portfolios. The trigger for deprioritize is the filtering pattern from the bad signals.
